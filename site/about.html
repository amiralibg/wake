About Wake

A new age of browsers.

Tab bars have looked the same for twenty years. The web hasn't. Wake starts from one idea: the page you open should sit next to the page you came from.

Principles

Five rules for a modern browser.

  1. 01

    Show the path.

    Every link opens beside the page you came from. To get back, you scroll left. There is no tab to hunt for, because nothing went into a tab.

  2. 02

    Remember the way people do.

    A bookmark keeps an address. A moment keeps your scroll position, the words you selected and a note on why. Wake checks saved pages in the background and tells you what changed.

  3. 03

    Get out of the way.

    The toolbar floats as glass and appears when you reach for it. Zen hides it completely. The page gets the window.

  4. 04

    Be a real Mac app.

    SwiftUI and AppKit for the interface, WebKit for the pages. No Chromium, no Electron, no second browser engine bundled inside.

  5. 05

    Work in the open.

    MIT licensed. The code, the issues and every release are on GitHub, and updates are signed so an installed copy only accepts real ones.

The name

A page, and the wake it leaves.

The icon is a page moving forward, with slimmer, fainter copies of itself trailing behind. That's the trail of columns, and it's where the name comes from.

The first-launch tour tells the same story on live water: a Metal shader, a paper boat, and the wake behind it. The download page runs a WebGL port of that shader.

What Wake is made of

SwiftUIAppKitWebKitMetalSparkleSwift 6SwiftUIAppKit

Made from Apple's own frameworks. Pages render in WebKit, the engine behind Safari, so sites behave the way they do there. Wake runs on macOS 14 and later, and uses Liquid Glass on macOS 26.

Who's building it

Built by amiralibgi, in the open.

Wake is written in Swift, one feature per folder, and every commit is public. If a site breaks in Wake, open an issue. If you want to see what's coming next, follow along on X.
